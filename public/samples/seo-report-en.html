UrgentSpeed and mobile

5 pages don't adapt to phones

What this means: On a phone, these pages show as they would on a computer, shrunk down: people have to zoom to read and to tap buttons. Most of your customers come from phones, and Google judges your site on its mobile version.

What to do: Ask whoever runs your site to add the “viewport” setting that fits the page to the phone's screen. Every recent WordPress theme has it: its absence often means a very old theme or a hand-made page.

Pages affected (5)

  • /
  • /contact
  • /livraison
  • /freins
  • /filtres
For your developer

No <meta name="viewport"> in the <head>. Add <meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1">. Without it, mobile browsers lay the page out ~980 px wide, then shrink it.

ImportantInformation for Google

Your homepage doesn't tell Google who you are

What this means: Google reads an “ID card” hidden in the page's code: name, logo, address, phone. Without it, it has to guess, and may confuse you with another business of the same name. AI assistants may use it too, but none has confirmed it: it's first of all for Google.

What to do: Ask whoever runs your website to add this ID card to the homepage, with your name, logo, address, phone and opening hours. On WordPress, an SEO plugin (Yoast, Rank Math) does it.

Pages affected (1)

  • /
For your developer

No JSON-LD block (or microdata) of type Organization, LocalBusiness or a subtype, or Person, on the homepage. Add an Organization block (or LocalBusiness / the precise subtype if customers visit in person) with name, url, logo, address (PostalAddress), telephone, sameAs and, for a shop, openingHoursSpecification. Check it with Google's Rich Results Test.

Check S03 · business-identity-missing

Should fixVisibility in Google

Your homepage leads to no other page of your site

What this means: Google, AI assistants and your visitors discover your pages by following the links on the homepage. Yours has no link to another page of the site: its links go to sections of the same page, to other sites (social networks, WhatsApp) or nowhere (see the example). If your site has other pages, nobody can find them from the homepage, and without a sitemap neither can Google. If your site is deliberately a single page, ignore this point.

What to do: Add real links to your other pages (products, services, contact…) in the menu or at the bottom of the page, and a sitemap declared in Google Search Console.

For your developer

No <a href> to another URL of the same site in the homepage's served HTML, nor (when Chrome could read it before the crawl) once its JavaScript has run. Buttons without href, JavaScript handlers and client-side routers that switch views without links aren't followed by crawlers. Serve real <a href> links and an XML sitemap.
